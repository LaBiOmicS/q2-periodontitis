# Software Engineering & Technical Specification: `q2-periodontitis`

**Bioinformatics Plugin for the QIIME 2 Ecosystem**  
**Focus:** Subgingival Biofilm Ecological Characterization, Projection into Socransky Complexes, and Periodontal Dysbiosis Indices

---

## 1. Biological Context & Problem Statement

### 1.1. The Analytical Bottleneck in Oral Metagenomics
Periodontitis is a multifactorial chronic inflammatory disease associated with polymicrobial dysbiosis in the subgingival biofilm. Standard amplicon (16S rRNA) or shotgun metagenomic workflows in the QIIME 2 ecosystem typically generate:
1. Feature count tables (`FeatureTable[Frequency]`);
2. Hierarchical taxonomic classifications (`FeatureData[Taxonomy]`);
3. Global alpha (Shannon, Faith's PD) and beta (Bray-Curtis, UniFrac) diversity metrics.

While foundational, these outputs are agnostic to oral pathophysiology. In translational research and clinical periodontology, pure taxonomic profiling fragments interpretation across hundreds of sparse features, failing to translate data into the microbial succession and classic functional architecture described in the dental literature.

### 1.2. The Succession Model & The Keystone-Pathogen Theory
Historically, **Socransky et al. (1998)** established that subgingival biofilm organizes into mutualistic, temporal bacterial consortia ("Socransky Complexes"). Subsequently, the **Keystone-Pathogen Hypothesis (Hajishengallis et al., 2012)** and contemporary metagenomic analyses demonstrated that low-abundance keystone pathogens (such as *Porphyromonas gingivalis*) orchestrate tissue homeostasis breakdown, triggering an overgrowth of proteolytic anaerobes.

`q2-periodontitis` resolves this bottleneck by providing a reproducible algorithmic framework in QIIME 2 that:
* Automatically maps taxonomic strings from **any reference database** (including **eHOMD**, **SILVA**, **Greengenes2**, **GTDB**, **NCBI**, as well as consensus taxonomy generated by `q2-consensus-taxonomy`) to their corresponding functional ecological complexes;
* Mathematically aggregates multidimensional ASV matrices into collapsed complex representations (`SampleData[SocranskyComplexes]`);
* Computes analytical dysbiosis and pathogenic risk indices (pMDI, pMDI_clr, SWL, and KRS);
* Supports longitudinal studies and Pre- vs. Post-treatment therapeutic monitoring (Delta pMDI, Delta SWL);
* Renders interactive visual reports in `.qzv` format with integrated non-parametric statistical testing (Kruskal-Wallis / Mann-Whitney) and standardized color palettes.

## 2. Mathematical and Computational Formalization

### 2.1. Algebraic Definitions
Let **X** be an observed feature count matrix of *N* samples and *M* features:

$$\mathbf{X} = [x_{i, j}]_{N \times M}, \quad x_{i, j} \ge 0$$

To mitigate sequencing depth bias, define the relative frequency matrix **P**:

$$p_{i, j} = \frac{x_{i, j}}{\sum_{k=1}^M x_{i, k}}$$

Let $\mathcal{F} = \{f_1, f_2, \dots, f_M\}$ be the set of features, and let $T: \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{S}_{\text{tax}}$ be the function mapping each feature to its taxonomic string.

Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the set of ecological complexes defined by the model:

$$\mathcal{K} = \{\text{Red}, \text{Orange}, \text{Yellow}, \text{Green}, \text{Purple}, \text{Blue}, \text{Extended}, \text{Other}\}$$

Define the hierarchical mapper function $C: \mathcal{S}_{\text{tax}} \to \mathcal{K}$.

### 2.2. Linear Projection and Complex Aggregation
The mapper partitions $\mathcal{F}$ into $K = |\mathcal{K}|$ disjoint subsets:

$$\mathcal{F}_c = \{f_j \in \mathcal{F} \mid C(T(f_j)) = c\}$$

Construct the binary projection matrix **W**:

$$W_{j, c} = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } C(T(f_j)) = c \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

The collapsed complex relative abundance matrix **Y** is computed via matrix multiplication:

$$\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{P} \mathbf{W}$$

Where each element $y_{i, c}$ represents the total relative abundance of complex $c$ in sample $i$.

### 2.3. Dysbiosis and Pathogenic Risk Indices Formulation

#### 2.3.1. Periodontal Microbial Dysbiosis Index (pMDI)
The **pMDI** quantifies the log-ratio between pro-inflammatory/destructive taxa and early commensal colonizers associated with periodontal health:

$$pMDI_i = \log_{10} \left( \frac{\sum_{c \in \mathcal{D}} y_{i, c} + \epsilon_i}{\sum_{c \in \mathcal{H}} y_{i, c} + \epsilon_i} \right)$$

Where:
* $\mathcal{D} = \{\text{Red}, \text{Orange}, \text{Extended}\}$ (dysbiotic subset);
* $\mathcal{H} = \{\text{Yellow}, \text{Blue}\}$ (eubiotic/commensal subset);
* $\epsilon_i$ is a sample-wise dynamic adaptive pseudocount defined as:

$$\epsilon_i = \begin{cases} \frac{\min(y_{i, c} > 0)}{2}, & \text{if } \exists \, y_{i, c} > 0 \\ 10^{-5}, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

##### Compositional Variant (pMDI_clr)
For analyses adhering to Compositional Data Analysis (CoDA) principles:

$$pMDI_{\text{clr}, i} = \text{clr}(y_{i, \mathcal{D}}) - \text{clr}(y_{i, \mathcal{H}})$$

##### Interpretation:
* `pMDI >> 0`: Severe dysbiosis / pathogenic dominance (mature biofilm, active periodontal pockets).
* `pMDI ~ 0`: Transitional community / mild inflammation.
* `pMDI << 0`: Commensal dominance / periodontal homeostasis.

#### 2.3.2. Socransky Weighted Load (SWL)
The **SWL** computes pathogenic risk weighted by the virulence and proteolytic potential of each cluster:

$$SWL_i = \sum_{c \in \mathcal{K}} \omega_c \cdot y_{i, c}$$

Calibrated Empirical Virulence Weights ($\vec{\omega}$):
* $\omega_{\text{Red}} = 1.00$ (High virulence and proteolysis)
* $\omega_{\text{Extended}} = 0.90$ (Contemporary rapid-progression pathogens)
* $\omega_{\text{Orange}} = 0.60$ (Co-aggregation bridge and moderate anaerobes)
* $\omega_{\text{Green}} = 0.20$ (Facultative secondary colonizers)
* $\omega_{\text{Purple}} = 0.10$ (Intermediary byproduct consumers)
* $\omega_{\text{Yellow}} = 0.00$ (Primary commensals)
* $\omega_{\text{Blue}} = 0.00$ (Primary commensals)
* $\omega_{\text{Other}} = 0.00$ (Unclassified background)

#### 2.3.3. Keystone Risk Score (KRS)
To capture the triggering effect of low-abundance pathogens per the *Keystone-Pathogen Hypothesis*, **KRS** introduces a sigmoidal penalty when Red complex abundance exceeds a critical threshold:

$$KRS_i = \left( 1 + \tanh\left( \alpha \cdot \frac{y_{i, \text{Red}}}{\theta_{\text{Red}}} \right) \right) \cdot \left( y_{i, \text{Red}} + y_{i, \text{Extended}} + 0.5 \cdot y_{i, \text{Orange}} \right)$$

Where $\theta_{\text{Red}} = 0.005$ (0.5%) represents the empirical critical threshold for keystone-induced dysbiosis, and $\alpha$ controls sigmoidal steepness.

## 3. Taxonomy and Database Agnostic Reference

### 3.1. Agnostic Mapping Strategy and Assignment Modes
`q2-periodontitis` operates agnostically regarding reference taxonomy databases (**eHOMD**, **SILVA**, **Greengenes2**, **GTDB**, **NCBI**), as well as consensus taxonomy generated by `q2-consensus-taxonomy`.

#### 3.1.1. Resolution Modes (`--p-assignment-mode`)
1. `strict`: Requires exact species-level (`s__`) match. Unresolved genus-level ASVs fall into `Other` or `Unresolved_Genus`.
2. `genus-majority` (Default): Resolves at the genus level (`g__`) for genera with conserved oral complex associations (e.g., *Tannerella* -> Red; *Fusobacterium* -> Orange; *Veillonella* -> Purple; *Actinomyces* -> Blue).
3. `probabilistic`: Uses reference database species relative distributions to proportion ASV abundance across matching complexes.
